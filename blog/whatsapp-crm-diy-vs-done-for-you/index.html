WhatsApp CRM: DIY Software or Done-For-You? The Honest Total-Cost Comparison

24 November 2026 · By The Growth Door, Chennai · 6 min read

Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales

Search for the best WhatsApp CRM for a small business in India and you will find two very different offers wearing the same name. One is software you buy and set up yourself. The other is a system someone builds and runs for you. Both call themselves a WhatsApp CRM, and the sticker prices look nothing alike, which is why so many owners pick the cheaper one and then wonder why nothing changed. This guide compares the two routes honestly, on total cost rather than sticker price, so you can choose the one that actually gets used in your business.

The two routes, in plain words

DIY software means you buy a monthly subscription to a platform, connect your own number to the official WhatsApp Business Platform, build your own pipeline, write your own template messages, and train your own team. Wati is one of the best known DIY platforms in India. You get the tool, and the work of making it useful is yours.

Done-for-you means a provider does that work for you. The number is connected, the shared inbox is built, the pipeline stages and labels are set up, the quick replies and templates are prepared, and your team is trained. Then the provider keeps the follow-up system running month to month. That is the model our WhatsApp CRM system follows, built on the official WhatsApp Business Platform (Meta API).

What DIY software really costs

For the software alone, Indian small businesses in 2026 typically see DIY platform subscriptions roughly in the Rs. 1,000 to Rs. 2,500 per month range for starter and growth plans, with higher tiers above that. A subscription at Rs. 1,500 a month is Rs. 18,000 in a year, before a single hour of your time is counted, and before Meta's own message charges, which are billed separately on your card whatever route you take.

Your time is the part nobody quotes. Connecting the number to the official API, learning the tool, designing the pipeline, writing templates and getting them approved, training staff, and fixing things when they break: that is a week of evenings for an owner, or several days of a staff member's time. The tool is bought, the number is connected halfway, and three months later the team is back on personal WhatsApp because nobody finished the setup. The subscription was cheap. The outcome was not. We wrote about that leak in why your business is losing WhatsApp leads.

What done-for-you really costs

With a done-for-you service you pay a one-time setup fee plus a monthly management fee, and the setup work is included in the price. Our own pricing, stated plainly as one concrete reference point: Setup Rs. 4,999 plus Rs. 1,999 per month. Meta's message charges stay separate, billed by Meta directly on your own card, with no markup and no platform fee added by us. The full breakdown is in our guide to what a WhatsApp CRM costs in India.

The first-year arithmetic is simple. A DIY subscription at Rs. 1,500 a month is Rs. 18,000 for the year, plus your setup hours. Our done-for-you pricing is Rs. 4,999 once plus Rs. 1,999 a month, which is Rs. 28,987 in the first year, then Rs. 23,988 a year after that. The DIY route is cheaper on paper. The question that decides it is whether the system is actually running, with follow-ups happening, in month three.

The costs no quote shows you

When you compare the two routes, these are the lines that decide the real total:

  • Your time. DIY setup is your evenings or your staff's days. Done-for-you buys that time back.
  • The half-finished setup. A platform nobody finished configuring costs the full subscription and delivers nothing.
  • Follow-up discipline. A system only pays when leads are followed up on day two and day five, not just answered on day one. That needs reminders and stages, not memory.
  • Templates and the 24-hour rule. After a customer goes quiet for 24 hours, only an approved template restarts the chat. Someone has to write those templates and get them approved. We explain the rule in the 24-hour WhatsApp rule, explained.
  • Training and staff changes. When a new staff member joins, someone has to show them the system. With a managed service, that training is part of the deal.
  • Who fixes it when it breaks. A number disconnect, a template rejection, a label that stopped working: on DIY, that is your problem to solve. On done-for-you, it is the provider's.
  • Ownership. Your number, your contacts and your history should remain yours, with no lock-in, whichever route you take. Ask before you sign anything.

The honest limits, on both sides

No honest comparison hides the limits. Automated first replies are something we run in a test inbox today, not a promise on a client's main number yet, so do not buy any WhatsApp CRM for AI replies alone. Voice calls stay on your regular phone line while every chat is managed in the CRM: WhatsApp voice calling inside the CRM is not available on our setup today, so when a chat needs a call, your team dials the customer from the normal number while the full chat history stays in front of them. Template broadcasts are possible only with approved templates, and Meta's per-message charges apply to them, so any provider promising campaign results should be able to show campaigns that actually ran.

Which route fits which business

DIY fits a business with someone in-house who enjoys configuring software and has real hours to give it. If that person exists, and they will still be there in three months, DIY is genuinely the cheaper route.

Done-for-you fits the far more common case: a small team, an owner-run business, or anyone whose follow-up currently depends on memory. If nobody in the business has a spare week to build the system, paying for the setup is not a luxury. It is the difference between owning a CRM and renting a login nobody opens.

How to decide in ten minutes

Ask yourself these four questions before you pay anyone:

  • Who in my business will do the setup, and when, in real hours?
  • Who will follow up with leads on day two and day five, and what reminds them?
  • Are Meta's charges passed through at cost, on my own card, with no markup?
  • Can I see the system working on my own kind of enquiries before I pay?

If you can answer all four, DIY may serve you well. If the first two have no name attached, the cheaper sticker price is the more expensive choice. Questions owners ask us

Short answers, before you message us.

What is the best WhatsApp CRM for a small business in India?

It depends on who does the work. If someone in your business has the time and interest to set up and maintain a platform, a DIY subscription is the cheapest route. If nobody has that time, a done-for-you service is usually the better value, because the system actually gets built, used and followed up on.

Is Wati a good alternative to a done-for-you WhatsApp CRM?

Wati is one of the well-known DIY WhatsApp CRM platforms in India, and it suits businesses that want to configure the tool themselves. The difference is not the software, it is who does the setup, the pipeline, the templates, the training and the monthly follow-up discipline. Compare the two routes on total cost, including your own hours, not on the monthly subscription alone.

Who pays Meta's WhatsApp message charges?

You do, directly. Meta bills your own card at actuals, and replies inside the 24-hour service window are mostly free. With us there is no markup and no platform fee added on top of Meta's charges, whichever route you choose.

How much does each route cost in the first year?

At the prices in this guide: a DIY subscription at Rs. 1,500 a month is Rs. 18,000 for the year, plus your own setup hours. Our done-for-you pricing is Rs. 4,999 once plus Rs. 1,999 a month, which is Rs. 28,987 in the first year. Meta's message charges are separate on every route, billed on your own card.

Do I need a new phone number for a WhatsApp CRM?

No. Your existing business number can be connected to the official WhatsApp Business Platform, and your customers keep messaging the same number they already know. What changes is where the chats land: in a shared team inbox instead of one person's phone.

Can my whole team reply from one number?

Yes. A WhatsApp CRM is a shared inbox, so several team members can see and reply to chats on the same business number, with labels and stages showing who is handling what. Nobody has to pass a phone around or forward screenshots.

Can I send offers and broadcasts to my customers?

Broadcasts go out only as approved template messages, and Meta charges per template message on your own card. Any provider who promises campaign results should be able to show you campaigns that actually ran, so ask for that before you pay.

What happens to my number and chats if I stop the service?

They stay yours. Your number, your contacts and your chat history belong to your business, whichever route you take. Ask any provider, including us, to confirm there is no lock-in before you sign.

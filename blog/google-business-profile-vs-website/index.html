Google Business Profile vs a Website: Which Brings a Chennai Business More Enquiries?

17 November 2026 · By The Growth Door, Chennai · 6 min read

Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales✦ Leads → Growth → Sales

A Chennai shop owner asked us a simple question: if I can only do one thing this month, should I set up my Google Business Profile properly, or build a website? It is a fair question, because both cost time and money, and both promise enquiries. The honest answer is that they do different jobs, and the right order depends on where your enquiries come from today.

The short answer

Your Google Business Profile brings the first enquiries faster, because it puts your business on Google Maps and in the local results people see when they search for what you sell. A website brings enquiries you own and control, from people who want detail before they call. For most Chennai small businesses, the profile comes first and the website follows within weeks, not months, because each one makes the other work harder. Neither replaces the other.

What a Google Business Profile actually does

A Google Business Profile is your free listing on Google Search and Maps. When someone nearby searches for a salon near me or a cake shop in Tharamani, your profile is what shows your name, photos, timings, phone number, reviews and directions. It is built for one moment: a person nearby, ready to call or visit, deciding between you and the next listing. For a business that depends on walk-ins and calls, it is the fastest way to be found. What it cannot do is tell your full story. You get a short description, a few posts and photos, and the layout is Google's, not yours. You cannot run a proper enquiry form on it, and you cannot control what a visitor sees first. Verification also takes time: Google asks you to prove the business exists, usually by postcard or video, and until that step clears, your profile stays limited. We know this first hand, because our own profile has been waiting on verification since late October. Our Google Business Profile setup page explains what a complete profile includes.

What a website actually does

A website is the one place online that is fully yours. It carries your services, your prices, your photos, your story and your enquiry form, in the order you choose. When a customer finds you on Google and wants to know more before calling, the website is where they land. It also works for the searches a profile cannot win: service plus area searches, price questions and comparison searches, where a detailed page ranks and a short listing does not. A website also protects you from platform changes. If Google changes how listings look, your profile changes with it. Your website stays as you built it. The trade off is cost and time: a proper 5 to 7 page business website takes a week or two to build, and it needs someone to keep it updated. Our business website page shows what a 5 to 7 page site includes.

Side by side: the honest comparison

Here is how the two compare on the points that matter to an owner:

  • Cost: the profile is free to create and manage. A website is a one-time build cost, and our business website is Rs. 9,999 one-time for 5 to 7 pages, with domain and hosting extra at actuals only if you do not already have them. We wrote the full price bands in what a website costs in Chennai.
  • Speed to first enquiry: the profile can bring calls within days once it is verified. A website takes weeks, because it has to start ranking first.
  • Control: on the profile, Google decides the layout and the rules. On a website, you decide everything.
  • Best for: the profile wins nearby searches, calls, directions and walk-ins. The website wins research, comparison, bookings and orders.
  • Trust: the profile builds trust through reviews and a Maps presence. The website builds trust through detail, photos, proof and a proper enquiry form.
  • Risk: one policy change or a suspended listing can hide your profile overnight. A website is yours, but nobody finds it unless search or links send people there.

When a profile alone is enough

If you are a single-location business, most of your customers come from within a few kilometres, and your service is simple to explain in one line, a complete profile plus a WhatsApp number can carry you for a while. A tea stall, a tailor, a small salon with a loyal street: the profile plus prompt replies may be all you need at the start. The moment you want customers from beyond your neighbourhood, or you sell something people research before buying, the profile alone starts to leak enquiries.

The order that works for most Chennai businesses

Here is the order we recommend, and the one we follow for our own clients. First, claim and complete your Google Business Profile: correct name, category, timings, photos, services and a WhatsApp link. Second, build a simple website that answers the questions your customers actually ask, with an enquiry form and a WhatsApp button. Third, link the two: your profile points to your website, and your website carries your Maps link and your reviews. Fourth, set up follow-up, because both channels bring enquiries, and enquiries slip away when nobody tracks them.

How to decide for your business

Use this as a quick check:

  • Most customers within a few kilometres and you need calls this week: profile first.
  • Customers compare and research before they buy: website first, profile alongside it.
  • You already get enquiries but lose track of them: fix follow-up before spending on either.
  • You have neither: profile this week, website next, follow-up from day one.

Questions owners ask us

Short answers, before you message us.

Is a Google Business Profile free?

Yes. Creating and managing a profile costs nothing. Google may charge for optional ads, but the listing itself is free, which is why it is the right first step for most local businesses.

Can a Google Business Profile replace a website?

For a very local business, for a while. But a profile cannot carry detailed service pages, prices or an enquiry form, and you do not control the layout, so most businesses outgrow it within months.

Do I still need a website if my profile ranks well?

Yes, because the profile brings the click and the website closes the enquiry: detail, proof, prices and a form, all in one place you own. The profile and the website do different jobs, and they work best together.
